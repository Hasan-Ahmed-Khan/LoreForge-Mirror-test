The far rim came up on the forty-fifth day: a red line, then a shelf of rock with a wall on it, then the smell of a town that boils what it cannot sell. Tallow Reach has no quays. The wagons went up the shelf one at a time on a winch, and the crust let go of the wheels for the last time, and the world had a far side after all, and it was a poor one.

They weighed at the outbound bridge under the wall.

A hundred and twenty-two blocks short. Then Ysoly Tamm took a yard of black rim-oak from under her coat and laid it on the table, and the clerk turned it in his hands and read the four faces without being told which was which, because a weighbridge clerk has read a hundred of them.

Heads. Water drawn. Ballast. Debt.

"Twenty-two at Sixty Wells, eleven at the Dry Ferry, a hundred laid off at the centre," he said. "And this."

"Read it out."

"'One thousand and eighty gallons out of the ground at Benchmark One, on the twenty-third day of the summer season, drawn four times to the clay and recovered four times, at two hundred and seventy the draw.'" He looked up. "That is not a weighbridge matter."

"No."

"I will enter the ballast and the debt. Under the fifth head this stick is as good as a ledger and I will not argue with a notch. I cannot enter water that never went on a scale."

"Then write that I asked you to."

He looked at her a moment, and then did it, in the margin of the day's sheet: that the master of the crossing had asked, and had been told it was not a weighbridge matter. Ysoly thought about a woman on brown ground promising to enter that a sentence had been spoken too late, and about a figure called at dawn to nobody so that afterwards no one could claim it had not been said; and she put the stick back inside her coat.

The Level House stands three streets up where the shelf rock comes to the surface: one hall and a short tower of dressed stone under a lead roof, the only building in the reach with a face on it. Inside it is cold, and after forty-five days of hide and glare the cold came up through the boots. A datum is scribed across the floor, a slit cut in the north wall to let a stripe of noon in, and the place smells of stone, lamp-black and, faintly, tallow, because the pupils lodge over the rendering sheds and so does the roll.

Denet Rasca carried the packet up the three streets himself, set it on the roll room table, and cut Pell Corrow's pale thread with a knife.

The keeper of the roll was a woman of seventy with ink to the second knuckle, and she heard it all standing up: two summers, eleven bores, nine of them wet, the glass floated against the far-rim reference, the packet stitched in the spring, nothing entered, no fair copy anywhere, five months of carrying it.

"You know the rule."

"I know the rule. I drafted the entry twice and sent neither. I am asking to make it now."

"You are five months late and the ground is sealed."

"Yes."

"Then make it," she said, "because the roll takes the finding and not the man," and opened the leaf where there had been no line against his name since the spring, and gave him the pen.

Four lines. Surveyor, ground, date, one line of result: fresh water under the crust at Benchmark One, nine wet bores of eleven, struck between nine feet and fourteen, read fresh against the far-rim reference.

Then he asked for a fifth, and sent to the yards for Ysoly Tamm.

She came up in her salt-stiff coat and stood on cold stone holding a yard of notched oak while the keeper read a caravan master's water face aloud and wrote the volume into the College's book. Denet said the fifth head of the Compact for her, twice: a tally cut by the master of a licensed crossing, in the presence of her own hands, is a book of account within the bounds, admissible at either rim.

"She was licensed when she cut it," he said. "It was cut on the morning of the twenty-third. The tile went at dusk."

"Then the volume goes in," said the keeper, and it went in.

A boring gives a depth and a quality and cannot give a volume. By that afternoon the roll carried both, more than any finding on the Meniscus had ever carried, and the forty-one leaves went into the press with the rest.

The depot bag came up from the yards at the eighth hour with the mail off the joint tile, and in it were six lines in a small institutional hand, dated at the Dry Ferry: that Denet Rasca, sealed of the College of Levels, had surveyed the plain's centre, sealed his findings in the spring, entered nothing on the roll, and had carried the packet on his person since.

Not one thing in it was untrue.

The College sat that evening under the meridian slit, and its statutes left it nothing to decide. A seal is what the College owns. A sealed man who files nothing is a man with a lead stamp, and the remedy is to lift it. Denet Rasca put his stamp on the table before anybody asked for it, which the keeper said afterwards was the only part of the business she had any comfort in.

He worked it out later on the back of a chandler's bill, because he could not be in a room with two times and a pencil and leave them apart. The entry had gone in a little after the second hour. The bag had come up at the eighth. Four hours and some, at the end of five months and eight hundred miles.

He had won it. It was the one sequence in the whole business that went his way, and the finding stands on the roll where anybody may read it, beside a note in a second hand recording that the surveyor's seal was lifted.

To argue with a line of it a man would have to go and stand on the ground and put an instrument down. The ground is held in trust by the Wardenry until the hazard is surveyed and lifted, and the Wardenry appoints the surveyor.
