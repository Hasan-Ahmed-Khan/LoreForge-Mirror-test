She put the cup down on the stone.

"Carry him. And when we come onto the brown ground on the twentieth day, put a bar in it."

Vesh said, "Onn—"

"Day twenty. I have walked over that ground thirty-one times with brine in the butts, and I went round the soft patches, because the soft patches are what kill you, and I was right every time." Her voice did not change at any point. "In the year sixty-six I filled at Sixty Wells off a board a man had chalked at first light. My crossing went south past this rock with what it had, and on the twentieth day out we buried four people. I have thought about the twentieth day every year since, and until six days ago I thought I was thinking about thirst.

"If that bag is right, they went down on top of fourteen feet of sweet water with a spade in the wagon. They did not die of the plain. They died of nobody being able to read the ground.

"So. Paper can be bought, and it can be burned, and a man's seal can be taken off him eight hundred miles away by somebody who never leaves a counting-house. Nobody has yet worked out how to buy a hole with water standing in it. The joint tile is four days behind us the whole way — thirty-six wagons, two hundred people, a taster of their own — and it comes through the Centre on the twenty-fourth day. Dig it before they get there, and let two hundred people walk past it and look down."

Nobody said anything for some while. The trough ran, and the third shift drank.

"Rasca," Ysoly said. "Inside the awning. Read her the Fourth Article. The first sentence, not your favourite part."

Denet did not have to open anything. "'Water rising or standing within the bounds is a hazard to the crossings and is held in trust by the Wardenry.'"

"Again."

"'Water rising or standing within the bounds.'"

"There is an argument," Ysoly said to the awning, "which the surveyor made in my cell at Sixty Wells and which I have carried seven days like a stone in a boot: that a lens nine feet under the crust is not water rising and is not water standing, that the Article does not reach it, and that a rim advocate could stand up and say so and not be laughed at." She let that sit. "Now put a bar in it. Dig your hole and let it fill, and by evening there is water standing inside the bounds of the Meniscus with a Warden of the Third Rank riding half a mile up the beacon line — and not one advocate at either rim could argue anything for you at all, because you will have built the thing the Article was written about with your own hands."

"They will seal it anyway," Sabra said.

"They may. It is a different word when we hand it to them."

"Then we hand it to two hundred people first."

"With Iseul Fane in front of us the whole way." Ysoly turned the lamp up a notch, so that people had to see each other properly. "That is the whole argument, and it has no floor under it, and I have been walking round the outside of it since the wells.

"Here is what I have got. The way-bill says Bell Harrow to the far rim, one man, his own water, no freight above what he carries. I wrote that, and I signed it under a Warden's hand with the tiles cooling on the rack behind me, and the bill is the only thing standing between me and being told at a weighbridge that I carried something I did not. I am not going to be a master who set a paying passenger down on a rock because a factor made her a good offer at the right hour of the night. He rides. That is not a count, it is not a kindness, and nobody has to like it."

Ottery said, "Master, I've changed. Carry him."

"Why."

Ottery looked across the awning at Pell Corrow, and then at his own boots. "Because of what was read out fifth."

"That is not a reason."

"No," said Ottery. "It isn't."

"Bris?"

"Same."

"Then it is four to set him down, five to carry him and two who will not say; an hour ago it was six and three; and at the Cold Well it will be something else again, and none of it has decided anything. What it has done is put every one of you on the record in front of ten other people, in your own voice. That does not come off." Ysoly stood up. "The Centre is seven days. Nobody puts a bar in that ground, nobody says the word water in front of the Warden, nobody writes to anybody, until I say it at the Centre with all of you standing there. Say it back."

"Nobody puts a bar in the ground until you say it at the Centre," Hanei said, word for word, before she could stop herself.

And then the rest of them said it, raggedly and at different speeds, eleven people under a hide awning at two in the morning saying one sentence back to a woman with a lamp — and Pell Corrow said it too, and nobody looked at him while he did.
