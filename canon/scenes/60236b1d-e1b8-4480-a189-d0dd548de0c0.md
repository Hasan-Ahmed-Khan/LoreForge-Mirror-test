The joint tile took an hour and a half to come onto the ground, which is what two hundred people look like from underneath.

Hanei watched it come in from the box of the fourth wagon. The Tamm Line had been rung out of Bell Harrow on nine strokes and nobody in the streets had stopped for it. This lot had had the better part of an hour of bell.

The master's name was Sild, and Ysoly Tamm walked out to meet him with a cut thong on her collarbone and nothing on the end of it.

She got four words in.

"Yes," Sild said.

"I have not—"

"You have not finished and I am not going to make you." He was sixty, and he had bought into another man's tile at twenty-six because he could not carry the risk alone. "Eleven hands, nine wagons, forty head and a hundred and ninety blocks of Assembly ballast, entered on my bill under my roll number, with the Warden's endorsement. You take my ration, my order of march and my dawn. Your water-master answers to mine."

"She will not like that."

"Mine will not like it either and neither of them gets a say." He looked past her at the hole. "Ysoly. You have stood in a yard at Bell Harrow and told me for thirty years that a master who buys into a joint tile is a woman renting out her own road. I have thought about that sentence in the dark more often than you would believe. I am not going to enjoy one minute of this, and I want it said between us before we start."

Iseul Fane wrote the endorsement on the tailgate of the fourth wagon and stamped it, and it took her four minutes, and it was the last lawful thing anybody ever did about the Tamm Line.

They came past the hole all morning, in ones and threes, the way people go to look at a thing there is nothing to be done about. Four feet square, nine feet down, two feet of water in it with the sky sitting on top, and six paces west a lead disc wired to an iron pin driven to the collar.

Sild put his hand on the pin and did not touch the disc.

"Anybody who takes a cup out of that walks to Tallow Reach," he said, to nobody in particular, in a voice that carried the length of a wagon train. "The Wardenry holds it in trust. I have two hundred people, one tile, and a woman standing forty feet from me with a cut thong at her neck, and that is the whole of my argument, and I have never had a better one."

Two hundred people looked down into fresh water at the centre of the Meniscus and got back on the boxes.

"The salt is goods," Iseul said, when Vesh asked her. "The seal lies upon the ground and the water and upon nothing else. Have it off my ground by noon."

So the last work the Tamm Line ever did as the Tamm Line was carrying a hundred blocks of another firm's ballast back between the axles they had come off three days before, in the flat white of the morning, in front of two hundred people with nothing else to watch. Ysoly cut the hundred off the debt face where the hands could see her do it. It was the only debt on that stick she would ever discharge.

The depot's south bag came off the joint tile's second wagon at noon, and Oreth Vask was standing over it with the flap open when Denet Rasca came across the ground to him.

He had the letter in his hand. Six lines, sealed, to the keeper of the roll at the Level House.

"I could take it out," Vask said.

"Yes."

"I have been standing here eleven minutes working out whether it helps me. Lift your seal and the Wardenry cannot appoint you; if it cannot appoint you it cannot begin; if it cannot begin, the season turns and the seal lifts of itself and the ground is free for ever." He turned the letter over. "So it does not help me. It helps you, and it ruins me a year sooner, and I have been standing here eleven minutes anyway."

He held it out.

Denet Rasca took his own destruction out of a man's hand and did not open it, and stood holding it about as long as a glass takes to settle.

Then he sat down on the tailgate with a borrowed pen and wrote four lines. Surveyor. Ground. Date. One line of result.

He read them aloud, because Ysoly was behind him and Ysoly did not like a man reading to himself. "'Denet Rasca, sealed of the College of Levels. The centre of the Meniscus at Benchmark One and eleven bores east of it. The seasons of two hundred and one to two hundred and three. Fresh water under the crust at between nine and fourteen feet, floated against the reference brine; nine bores of eleven.'"

"It is five months late," Ysoly said.

"It is five months late, and it is entered by a man whose seal comes off him in the same post, which makes it worth nothing to anybody who reads it." He folded his four lines, folded Vask's six inside them, put both in the south bag and closed the flap. "If the number does not go on the roll it never happened, and I have had five months of finding out what that is like."

He had the packet out after that, Pell Corrow's pale crooked thread still across the head of it, and cut the thread with the same knife he had used at Sixty Wells. Then he counted forty leaves off the top and squared them on his knee.

The last was folded in four and larger than the rest.

"Leaf forty-one. The water-table map." He put it in Ysoly's hand. "Forty leaves prove a thing to a body that keeps rolls. This one tells a person where to put a spade, and it is the only part that is any use to somebody who is not an institution. 'Papers are the goods of him that holds them, and go off the ground in his hand.'"

"I carry nothing I cannot name on a bill," Ysoly said.

"You have no bill."

She looked at it a while. Then she put it inside her coat, next to a yard of black rim-oak that had stopped being a book of account at last light on the twenty-third day.

They went north at the second hour of the afternoon, which is the hour nobody travels, because Iseul Fane had forty days to the roll and had decided to spend fifteen of them. Ysoly weighed their water herself, off her own butts, against a cylinder borrowed from the cook wagon: two people, fifteen days, sixty gallons and the grace on it. She did not hurry any part of it, because it was the last provisioning she would ever do and nobody there was going to say so.

Denet Rasca went up on a hask with forty leaves against his body under his coat and the brass-cornered case on a strap across it, and got half a sentence out.

"Don't," Ysoly said.

He didn't.
