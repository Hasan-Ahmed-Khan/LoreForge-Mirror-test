The front room had a table, the toll-book, a strongbox with two locks in the floor beneath it, and on a ring in the wall a chain with a ten-pound iron cylinder on the end of it — the same cylinder that hung in every counting-house and depot on the Road.

"The field is on ration from noon," Aske said. "One gallon the head the day, for the days of your leg, at the licence rate. Seventy-seven gallons for eleven heads to the Ferry. Anything over that is bought."

"And the forty-one?" Sabra said.

"Not heads."

Nobody in the room found that remarkable except Hanei, who found it remarkable twice and said nothing both times.

"Read me the article," Ysoly said.

Iseul Fane did not open the waxed sleeve. "'Every licensed crossing shall carry water to the measure of two gallons the head the day, and shall answer for it at the far rim. Every well within the bounds is the Wardenry's to open, to close and to ration.' Third Article, original to the year one."

"Both halves in the same four lines. It tells me what I must carry and it lets him tell me what I may draw, and there is no clause saying the second has to be the larger." Ysoly set the licence book on the table and did not open it. "Now say the part you are not saying."

"Two gallons the head the day is a floor for eleven people. The statute has never had a figure of any kind for a hask, and it will not get one this morning."

A hundred and seventy gallons in her butts. A seven-day leg. Eleven heads at two gallons was a hundred and fifty-four of it, answerable at the far rim against a scale, which left sixteen gallons of sweet water for forty-one animals.

"Sabra."

"Sixteen gallons is a third of a pint the head the day. That is not a ration, that is a rounding."

"They go nine days on brine and cake."

"They pull for eight and go down on the ninth, and that is a hask watered to fill leaving here. There is no rotten crust between here and the Kell worth a bar. I have cut two on that stretch in thirty years and neither is on our beacons."

"Then we buy. Warden. What is it."

"A block the hundredweight."

Pell Corrow stopped mending. He had been stitching a girth in the corner since the article, and the needle stopped and stayed stopped, which Ysoly heard from four feet away without turning round.

"It was a block the four hundredweight on your own board yesterday."

"The field was not on ration yesterday."

"The field is not on ration today either. Nine wells were sweet at first light and nine are sweet now. You have the same water you had. What you have not got is a board you can stand behind."

"That is a fair statement of it." Aske was not a clever man and was not pretending to be. "In four days there is a joint tile behind you. Thirty-six wagons, two hundred people, a taster of their own — and I will have to tell their taster that mine called a well sweet eleven hours before a stranger's glass called it brack. Two crossings. In this field."

"Two crossings in this field," Sabra said, "is how the last one was counted."

"I was seven years old when it happened and I have never once wiped that board without thinking about it. If you want to tell me the order that killed them was the Wardenry's and not the wells, I will tell you half the men at Bell Harrow think so and none of them will say it on a roll. And I will still ration you, because if I do nothing and it turns, the two hundred behind you are on my chalk."

Sabra did not answer that.

"Two hundred and twenty gallons," Ysoly said.

The room took a moment over it.

"Master—"

"On top of the seventy-seven. Four hundred and sixty-seven standing when we roll, and two hundred and ninety-seven and a half is the leg. Four days' grace to the Ferry."

"That is what I asked you for at the weighbridge and did not get."

"It is."

"And it is two thousand two hundred pounds of water at a block the hundredweight. Twenty-two blocks. Twenty-one blocks was the wheelwright and the quarter on the paper, and you said so yourself in the smoke with the weighmaster listening."

"Twenty-one was. This is twenty-two."

"Then I am telling you it is too much water."

Ysoly looked at her a while. "Say it again, out loud, so that you have heard it."

Sabra Onn shut her mouth and did not say it again.

"A passenger with a tube took a chalked board off a wall this morning. He was right about thirty-four before either of you opened your mouths — I watched you spit it and I watched him read it, and if you had not agreed I could not have told anybody which of you to believe. There is nothing at this station I could swear to at a weighbridge, and a master who cannot know buys water. Twenty-two blocks. Weigh it out."

They weighed it out, because water at a Wardenry post is weighed and not measured, and it took the better part of the afternoon, with Rusk made to stand where he could see the beam.

Twenty-two blocks of second-grade salt came off wagons three, five and eight and went into the store behind the post, and the wagons rode a little higher for it, which Pell mentioned to nobody at all. Aske entered it in the toll-book: roll number, date, twenty-two blocks against two hundred and twenty gallons at ration, for the Bell Harrow roll at the season's end.

"That goes north in the autumn."

"It goes on the roll. What the roll does after that is not mine."

"The Assembly's counting-house is two doors from your licence office."

"I know where it is, madam."

She signed. Then she went down the line in the last of the light and turned the tally-stick past heads, past water drawn, past ballast, to the fourth face, and cut twenty-two on it at the tailgate of the first wagon where the hands could watch her do it.

Denet Rasca stood at the corner of the post with the case under his arm and watched her cut it, and did not come closer, and went in.
